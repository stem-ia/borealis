## 3. Period-by-period plan

**Every period:** learning goal and success criteria on the board at the start (4.1). Flow minutes add to 55. "Board" = classroom touch screen. "Devices" = one group device. If devices fail, every step runs from the board with paper Decision Slips (section 8).

**Paper trail:** each province keeps a **Province Ledger** (one page per year). Each role writes one line per year: "We chose ___ because ___. The trade-off is ___." This is the main evidence for the summative.

## 4. Group roles

### Phase 1: province roles (4 per province)

| Role | Job card (student-facing) |
| --- | --- |
| **Premier** | You lead. Hear everyone, then make the final call. You speak for your province at the board. |
| **Finance Minister** | You hold the money. Set taxes and spending. Check every choice against the budget and the debt. |
| **Trade Minister** | You get what your province is short on. Make fair deals on the trade floor. Keep your promises. |
| **Citizens' Advocate** | You speak for the people. Read the local issue. Ask: who wins, who loses? Present the province report. |

**Rotation.** Roles move one seat clockwise after Year 2 and after Year 4. Each student holds three roles in Phase 1.

**Everyone stays active.** Each role writes one ledger line every year. The device rotates to a new holder each period.

### Phase 2: party and Parliament roles

| When | Role | Job card |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Caucus (P10-P12) | Party Leader | Members elect you. Run the caucus. Give the debate pitch. |
| | Platform Chair | Turn your party's ideas into 3 clear promises with a price tag. |
| | Campaign Manager | Plan where to campaign. Spend your actions wisely. |
| | Whip | Keep members on the party's message. Count votes before a vote. |
| | All members | You are a party member. You vote for the leader, the priorities, and your riding's candidate. |
| Government (P13-P15) | Prime Minister | You lead Cabinet. Pick ministers. Recommend 3 senators. |

## 7. Real-world connections

Facts for the teacher to mention in one or two sentences. Neutral wording. No current politicians. Check numbers against a current source before class.

| Stage | Period | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scarcity | 1 | Canada's provinces have very different resources: oil and gas, hydro power, farmland, fisheries, minerals. |
| Mixed economy | 3 | Canada is a mixed economy: public healthcare and schools, private businesses, and Crown corporations like Canada Post. |
| Taxes and spending | 4 | Healthcare is the largest single spending area for most provinces. The federal government sends money to provinces for health through the Canada Health Transfer. |
| Surplus | 5 | The federal government ran a surplus every year from the late 1990s until the 2008-09 recession. |
| Deficit and debt | 5, 7 | In the mid-1990s, the federal government cut spending to reduce large deficits. In 2020-21, during the COVID-19 pandemic, it ran the largest deficit in Canadian history. |
| Interest on debt | 7 | Each year the federal government pays billions of dollars in interest on its debt. That money cannot go to services. |
| Indicators | 6 | Statistics Canada reports CPI and unemployment every month. Inflation rose to about 8% in 2022. |
| Global events | 6 | The 2008 financial crisis and the 2014-15 oil price drop hit some provinces harder than others. Prairie droughts affect farm income and food prices. |
| Bridge | 9 | Equalization: the federal government pays provinces with less ability to raise revenue so they can offer similar services. Canadians debate how it should work. |
| Elections | 12, 13 | Elections Canada runs federal elections. Student Vote Canada runs practice elections in schools. In recent federal elections, about 6 or 7 in 10 eligible voters voted. |
| Majority / minority | 13 | Canada has had several minority governments in the last 20 years. |
| Opposition | 15 | In 2005 and 2011, minority governments lost key votes in the House, which led to elections. |
| Senate | 13, 14 | Senators are appointed on the Prime Minister's recommendation and can serve until age 75. |

## 10. Decisions made

1. Nine success criteria under three unit goals, one goal per organizing idea.
2. Phase 1 has six fiscal years (P2-P7); P8 is review and the Phase 1 check.
3. Mini-lessons come just before the game step that needs the vocabulary. In P9 and P13, students experience it first, then the mini-lesson names it.
4. Political system types (PK2, PK3) are taught through the "Four Ways to Decide" activity in the bridge.
5. Traditional economy is taught by neutral example (custom-based farming or fishing communities in history), not tied to any Indigenous people, per the brief's sensitivity note.
6. Roles rotate clockwise after Year 2 and Year 4.
7. Province Ledger on paper is the main summative evidence, so the summative works without the app.
8. Party choice by secret slip with first and second choice. The teacher balances parties to 6-8 members. Random assignment on request.
9. Teacher plays Governor General. PM recommends 3 senators who sit as independents.
10. Bill stages appear as a simplified Grade 9 preview, not assessed.
11. SC6 is assessed in the Phase 2 check only, to keep the summative to one page.
12. Oral summative is live with the teacher, not recorded, so no student audio is stored.
13. Real-world examples use Canada-wide facts and no party names until Escalation 1 is decided.
